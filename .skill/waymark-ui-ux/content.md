# Waymark — Tone of Voice & Copy Rules

## Voice

A confident, senior design partner: **clear, calm, minimal, human**. No
agency-speak, no hype, no filler.

> Waymark turns complex product ideas into clear, high-craft digital
> experiences — strategy through UI/UX, brand, and build.

## Rules

1. **Convert with calm, not pressure.** Lead with outcomes: "we designed a
   checkout that lifted conversion 18%", not "we deliver world-class solutions".
2. **Short sentences.** Paragraphs ≤ 3 sentences. Headlines ≤ 10 words.
3. **Be concrete.** Prefer numbers, tools, deliverables. Avoid "innovative",
   "seamless", "cutting-edge", "synergy".
4. **Show, don't tell.** Every claim of quality should point to a case study.
5. **One CTA per section**, phrased as what the visitor gets:
   "Book a discovery call", "Get a design proposal", "Ask about our process".
6. **Form labels** are human: Full name, Work email, Company, Service needed,
   Budget range, Tell us about your project.

## Style details

- **English:** British (`-ise`, `colour` if used, "customised").
- **Punctuation:** serial comma; `:` after heading labels.
- **Numbers:** `+971 55 896 5353`; prices `$5K – $10K`; years full `2026`.
- **Referencing the studio:** "Waymark" in prose; "waymarks.agency" for the
  domain; "Waymark's" possessive with apostrophe.
- **CTA casing:** sentence case ("Book a discovery call" — not "BOOK").

## Template: case study

```
Title: <client> — <what we did, e.g. "Web app redesign">
Sector: <e.g. Fintech>        Services: <e.g. UI/UX, Research, Development>
Year: <2025>                  Outcome: <1–2 concrete results, qualitative ok>

Challenge   — 2–4 sentences: business/user problem.
Approach    — 3–6 sentences: research → UX → UI → build; name methods.
Result      — bullet outcome(s) with metrics when known.
Services    — tags for filtering.
Quote (optional) — from client, 1–2 sentences.
```

## Template: service

```
Title / slug / icon.
Summary — 1–2 sentences, outcome-led (what the client gets).
Deliverables — 5–8 bullets, concrete (e.g. "Usability-tested prototypes").
Methodology — 3–4 steps: Discover → Design → Validate → Deliver.
FAQs — 3–5 real questions with 1–2 sentence answers.
CTA — service-specific.
```

## Anti-glossary (never use)

"world-class", "turnkey", "one-stop", "best-in-class", "solutions" as a noun
for services ("product design" not "digital solutions"), "leverage" (verb),
"synergy", "holistic" (unless discussing actual wholeness of service).

Exceptions: content quoted from clients or legal pages — keep as written.
